What is Sacred Harp Singing?

Sacred Harp singing is a tradition of sacred choral music that originated in New England and flourished in the American South. The name comes from The Sacred Harp, a tunebook first published in 1844.

It's not a performance. There's no audience and no choir director. We sit in a "hollow square," with singers on four sides, all facing the center. Each side corresponds to one of the four vocal parts: treble, alto, tenor (which carries the melody), and bass.

The music uses "shape notes" to help with sight-reading. Each note shape (triangle, oval, rectangle, diamond) corresponds to a syllable (Fa, Sol, La, Mi). We sing the songs first on the "shapes" to learn the tune, then sing the words.

The sound is powerful, full-voiced, and deeply moving. It's a living tradition, and all are welcome to join, regardless of musical experience or religious affiliation. Try it; you'll like it.

More Resources

Explore the wider world of Sacred Harp singing.

Fasola.org
The most comprehensive web resource for Sacred Harp singing. Includes lists of local singings, historical information, and more.
Warren Steel's Sacred Harp Pages
Immense amounts of all sorts of information on Sacred Harp, up to and including recipes for dinner on the grounds.
Sheet Music & Recordings (Bremen)
A website from Sacred Harp singers in Bremen, Germany. Contains sheet music and midi files for all parts of any song in the Sacred Harp.
Online Concordance
The 2025 edition of the Sacred Harp, fully searchable by keyword, poet, composer, meter, title, first line, or page.
A Plea For Participation
An essay by Ginnie Ely specifically directed at those who are new to Sacred Harp.
The Southern Harmony
The online index to The Southern Harmony, searchable by tune name or first line.
